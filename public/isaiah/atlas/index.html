The Nations of Isaiah
A Reader's Atlas

Isaiah preaches to the whole map. One nation can wear four or five different names, and one name can mean two different nations. This chart is meant to sit beside your Bible: when you meet a place name, find it here.

How to read the dials. Every small compass shows a nation's direction from Jerusalem — Isaiah's vantage point for the entire book. The solid dot is Jerusalem itself. One quirk to know: Assyria and Babylon lie east, but their armies marched the caravan route around the desert and always arrived from the north — which is why the prophets speak of danger "out of the north."

A quick example, close to home. A dial points the way you would travel to reach a place. Picture yourself standing in Kansas:

ColoradoWest
MissouriEast
OklahomaSouth
IowaNortheast

On the chart below, simply swap Kansas for Jerusalem — Isaiah's vantage point — and read every dial the same way.

I

The Covenant People — Two Kingdoms

After Solomon (about 930 BC) the nation split in two. This is the single most important fact for reading Isaiah, because the word Israel is now ambiguous: sometimes it means the northern kingdom only, sometimes the whole covenant people, north and south together. The same is true of Jacob. Context decides — but when Isaiah says Ephraim or Samaria, he always means the north.

Israel · the Northern Kingdom

Samaria (capital) · Galilee, Zebulun & Naphtali (9:1) · "the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan"

7–9 · 17 · 28:1–6
Reader's note
Ephraim was the dominant northern tribe, so its name stands for the whole kingdom — the way "Washington" can mean America. When Isaiah pronounces woe on "the drunkards of Ephraim" (28:1), he is talking about the north, not Judah.
Judah · the Southern Kingdom

1–5 · 7–8 · 22 · 28:7–29 · 29 · 36–39 · 40–66 throughout
Reader's note
Zion is the book's center of gravity — judged in the early chapters, besieged in the middle, comforted and glorified at the end. When Isaiah says "this people," he almost always means Judah, standing right in front of him.
Landmarks of the Land
Mountains, forests & plains used as images
The names
Lebanon cedar forests, N Carmel coastal ridge, NW Sharon fertile plain, W Bashan oak country, NE Valley of Achor 65:10
Reader's note
These are real regions, but Isaiah mostly uses them as symbols of glory and fruitfulness — when they "wither and mourn" (33:9) the land is under judgment; when the desert blooms "like the glory of Lebanon, the majesty of Carmel and Sharon" (35:2), salvation has come.
II

The Great Powers

Four superpowers frame the book. Assyria is the live threat of Isaiah's own lifetime; Babylon is the storm on the far horizon; Egypt and Cush are the tempting ally to the south — the "help" Judah keeps wanting to trust instead of the LORD.

Assyria
Far northeast · arrives from the north
Northern Iraq today · capital Nineveh
Also called
the rod of my anger 10:5 the hired razor 7:20 the River's flood 8:7–8 "the Assyrian"
Places & kings
Nineveh (37:37) · kings Sargon (20:1) & Sennacherib (36–37) · its trophy list: Calno, Carchemish, Hamath, Arpad (10:9); Gozan, Haran, Rezeph (37:12)
Key chapters
7–8 · 10 · 14:24–27 · 20 · 30–31 · 36–37
Reader's note
The empire that swallowed the north in 722 and stood at Jerusalem's wall in 701. Isaiah's shocking claim: Assyria is God's own tool — a rod in His hand — which will itself be judged for its arrogance (10:5–19).
Babylon
Far east · arrives from the north
Southern Iraq today · on the Euphrates
Also called
Chaldea / the Chaldeans 43:14; 47:1 daughter of Babylon 47:1 Shinar 11:11 the wilderness of the sea 21:1 glory of kingdoms 13:19
Places & figures
Merodach-baladan its king (39:1) · its gods Bel & Nebo (46:1) · its king taunted as "Day Star, son of Dawn" (14:12)
Key chapters
13–14 · 21 · 39 · 46–48
Reader's note
In Isaiah's lifetime Babylon was Assyria's rebellious province — but the book sees ahead: Babylon will destroy Jerusalem (586 BC) and carry Judah into exile, and chapters 40–55 speak into that exile with comfort. Babylon also becomes Scripture's standing symbol of proud human empire.
Egypt
Southwest · the Nile kingdom
Egypt today · the tempting ally
Also called
Rahab 30:7; 51:9 "Rahab who sits still" 30:7 the shadow of Egypt 30:2 that broken reed 36:6
Places within
Zoan & Hanes (19:11; 30:4) · Memphis (19:13) · Pathros, Upper Egypt (11:11) · the Nile & its canals (19:5–8) · the Brook of Egypt, its border (27:12)
Key chapters
19–20 · 30–31 · 36:6
Reader's note
Judah's politicians kept riding south to buy Egyptian chariots against Assyria; Isaiah calls the whole project trusting "Rahab who sits still" — a blustering sea-monster that never actually helps. Yet 19:18–25 ends with one of the Bible's most stunning promises: Egypt healed, worshiping, called "my people."
Cush
Far south · beyond Egypt
Nubia — today's Sudan / "Ethiopia" in older Bibles
Also called
Ethiopia KJV & older translations land of whirring wings 18:1 Seba 43:3
Figures
Tirhakah, king of Cush (37:9) — Cushite pharaohs ruled Egypt itself in Isaiah's day (the 25th Dynasty)
Key chapters
18 · 20 · 45:14
Reader's note
The tall, smooth-skinned people "beyond the rivers" (18:2) — the far edge of Isaiah's map. Because Cushite kings sat on Egypt's throne, "Egypt and Cush" travel together in these oracles (20:3–5). Even distant Cush brings tribute to Zion in the end (18:7).
Media & Elam
Far east · beyond Babylon
Iran today · Babylon's future destroyers
Also called
the Medes 13:17 Elam 21:2; 22:6 Kir 22:6 — with Elam
Key figure
Cyrus, named two centuries early (44:28; 45:1) — also "a bird of prey from the east" (46:11), the one "stirred up from the north/east" (41:2, 25)
Key chapters
13:17 · 21:2 · 41 · 44:28–45:13
Reader's note
God's answer to Babylon. The Medes and Persians take Babylon in 539 BC, and Cyrus — a pagan king whom God calls "my shepherd" and "his anointed" — sends the exiles home. Isaiah names him long before his birth as proof that God alone declares the end from the beginning.
III

The Neighbors

The small nations ringing Israel and Judah — sometimes enemies, sometimes tempting alliance partners, all addressed in the "oracles against the nations" (chapters 13–23). The message to each: no nation, however near or clever, is outside the LORD's rule.

Aram (Syria)
Northeast · capital Damascus
Syria today
Also called
Syria Damascus its capital, ch. 17 "Rezin" its king, 7:1 a smoldering stump 7:4
Key chapters
7–8 · 17
Reader's note
Aram and northern Israel teamed up to strong-arm Judah into their anti-Assyrian revolt (the crisis behind chapter 7 and the Immanuel sign). Isaiah dismisses the fearsome pair as "two smoldering stumps of firebrands" — nearly burnt out already. Assyria destroyed Damascus in 732 BC.
Philistia
West · the coastal plain
Gaza coast today
Also called
the Philistines 9:12 "Philistia, all of you" 14:29
Places within
Ashdod (20:1) — one of its five great cities
Key chapters
14:28–32 · 20
Reader's note
Israel's ancient coastal rival (Goliath's people, centuries earlier). Warned not to celebrate one Assyrian king's death — a worse "serpent" is coming (14:29).
Moab
East · across the Dead Sea
Central Jordan today
Places within
Ar & Kir (15:1) · Dibon, Nebo, Medeba (15:2) · Heshbon & Elealeh (15:4) · Zoar (15:5) · Kir-hareseth (16:7) · Sibmah & Jazer (16:8–9) · Sela (16:1)
Key chapters
15–16 · 25:10–12
Reader's note
Kin to Israel through Lot, and Ruth's homeland. The oracle is dense with town names because it traces refugees fleeing town to town — and remarkably, Isaiah weeps for Moab as he pronounces it (15:5; 16:9–11). Judgment, delivered in tears.
Edom
Southeast · the red-rock country
Southern Jordan today · descendants of Esau
Also called
Dumah 21:11 — a grim pun: "silence" Seir 21:11 Bozrah 34:6; 63:1 — its chief city
Key chapters
21:11–12 · 34 · 63:1–6
Reader's note
Esau's nation — the brother-people who cheered Jerusalem's fall. In chapters 34 and 63 Edom becomes the representative of all nations set against God: the figure striding from Bozrah with garments stained red (63:1–6) is the LORD returning from judgment.
Tyre & Sidon
Northwest · the Phoenician coast
Lebanon's coast today
Also called
daughter of Sidon 23:12 the merchant of the nations 23:3 the bestower of crowns 23:8
Trade partners
ships of Tarshish (23:1, 14) · Cyprus (23:1, 12) · Shihor & the Nile's grain (23:3)
Key chapters
23
Reader's note
The Wall Street of the ancient world — sea-trading cities whose merchants were princes. Their oracle targets not military pride but commercial pride: wealth, too, must bow. Astonishingly, Tyre's profits end up "dedicated to the LORD" (23:18).
The Desert Tribes
East & southeast · Arabia
Northern Arabia today · caravan peoples
The tribes
Kedar 21:16; 42:11; 60:7 Dedan 21:13 Tema 21:14 Nebaioth 60:7 Midian, Ephah & Sheba 60:6
Key chapters
21:13–17 · 42:11 · 60:6–7
Reader's note
Bedouin traders descended largely from Ishmael and Abraham's other sons. Judged in chapter 21 — but look where they reappear: in chapter 60 their camels and flocks come to Zion carrying gold and frankincense, praise on their lips. (Matthew's magi fit this picture.)
IV

The Far Horizon

The edges of the known world — which is exactly where Isaiah's gospel is headed. "It is too light a thing that you should… restore the preserved of Israel; I will make you as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth" (49:6).

Tarshish
Far west · across the Mediterranean
Likely southern Spain — the end of the sea
Watch for
ships of Tarshish 2:16; 23:1; 60:9
Key chapters
2:16 · 23 · 60:9 · 66:19
Reader's note
"Ships of Tarshish" means the biggest ocean-going vessels afloat — symbols of human reach and pride in 2:16, but in 60:9 the same great ships are ferrying Zion's children home with silver and gold. What pride built, grace repurposes.
The Coastlands
Every direction · the ends of the earth
Distant maritime peoples — "the isles" in older Bibles
Also called
the islands / isles the ends of the earth 41:5 Tubal & Javan (Greece) 66:19 Put & Lud 66:19
Key chapters
11:11 · 41:1, 5 · 42:4, 10 · 49:1 · 51:5 · 60:9 · 66:19
Reader's note
A favorite word of chapters 40–66: the unnamed far-off peoples who have never heard of the LORD. Note the trajectory — the coastlands wait for His law (42:4), are summoned to listen (49:1), and receive missionaries who "declare my glory among the nations" (66:19). This is the Great Commission in seed form.
V

Code Names & Poetry

Isaiah loves to rename. Some names are satire, some are grief, some are promises. When a strange name appears and no map will help, check here.
