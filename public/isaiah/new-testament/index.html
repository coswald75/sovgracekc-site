In the New Testament
Isaiah's Nations & the Gospel's Advance

Isaiah's map runs one direction: the nations stream in to Zion (2:2–4). The book of Acts runs the same map the other way: witnesses go out from Zion — "in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth" (Acts 1:8). That last phrase is lifted straight from Isaiah 49:6, and Paul claims the verse as his marching orders in Acts 13:47. The apostles read their travel itinerary out of Isaiah. This chart traces where each of Isaiah's nations shows up in the New Testament story.

How to weigh the markers. Not every connection carries the same weight, and it matters to read honestly:

Quoted The New Testament cites the Isaiah text directly, often with a fulfillment formula.
Echoed A clear allusion — the NT writer is deliberately drawing on Isaiah's language or scene.
Resonant The pattern rhymes, but Scripture itself doesn't draw the line. Enjoy it; don't build on it.

The gathering completes

Follow the three companions in order and the story completes itself. The Reader's Atlas mapped the nations; the Kinship Atlas showed they were family; this chart shows the family being gathered. The gathering finishes in Revelation — and John describes the finish in Isaiah's own words:

"The nations will walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it, and its gates will never be shut by day…" Revelation 21:24–25 — drawing directly on Isaiah 60:3, 11: "Nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising… your gates shall be open continually."

Around the throne stands "a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages" (Rev 7:9) — the Table of Nations, complete. Ephraim and Judah, Kedar's herdsmen and Tarshish's sailors, a Cushite eunuch with a name better than sons, Egyptians called "my people" — every dial on these charts, pointed home.

And note what remains unfinished. Isaiah 66:19 sends witnesses to peoples "that have not heard my fame" — a verse still in force. The atlas ends where the church's assignment begins.
