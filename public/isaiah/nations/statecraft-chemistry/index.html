The Chemistry of Nations

Numbers 23:9 · Judges 11:24 · Psalm 96:5

Nations have to cooperate. Trade has to cross borders, wells have to be shared, wars have to end in something. The ancient world had a working technology for all of it — and the covenant handed Israel a confession that broke the technology on purpose. This page explains the machine the next three pages watch Israel's kings misuse — and adds the third exposure to the series' diagram: what happens when triangles touch.

Exposure 3 — how nations bonded: corner to corner, lapis to lapis, gods co-signing. And the one triangle with a sealed shell.
Big idea

In a polytheistic world, nations bonded the way atoms do — through their outer shell, the religion corner: every treaty co-signed by both parties' gods, every alliance a mutual recognition that your gods and mine are real to each other. Properly held, Israel's confession dissolved every bonding site: a noble gas among the elements, "a people dwelling alone." Act III is the story of kings trying to fake a valence the covenant said did not exist.

IThe Problem Every Nation Has
Genesis 21:22–34 · 1 Kings 5:10–12

Start with sympathy for the kings. Cooperation between nations is not a sin; it is a necessity of creation. Wells sit on borders and both flocks are thirsty (Gen 21:25–31). Timber grows in Lebanon and the temple site is in Jerusalem (1 Kgs 5). Caravans have to cross three kingdoms to reach a fourth; harvests fail on one side of a river and not the other; and every war, however it starts, has to end in words two enemies can both trust. The question was never whether nations would bind themselves to each other. The question was what could make a promise stick between two kings who each answered to no one on earth.

The ancient answer: someone above both thrones must witness the oath. A treaty was not a document between governments; it was a sworn covenant before the gods — and its last section was a witness list. Surviving Hittite treaties close with dozens of named deities, drawn from both pantheons, stacked like signatures, followed by curses on whichever king breaks faith. The gods were the enforcement mechanism. No gods, no contract law.

IIHow the Bond Worked
Judges 11:24 · Isaiah 36:18–20 · 2 Kings 5:17

For the mechanism to run, each nation's gods had to be real to the other nation — and in the polytheistic world they were, effortlessly. Jephthah negotiates with Ammon in flawless diplomatic protocol: "Will you not possess what Chemosh your god gives you to possess?" (Judg 11:24) — mutual recognition, god to god, portfolio to portfolio. Pantheons even had exchange rates: the storm-rider worshiped as Hadad in Damascus was Baal up the coast — same god, local branding — so two nations could often discover their gods were the same gods under different names. Shared electrons. A natural chemistry. On the series’ map: two triangles touching, and the bond always forged corner to corner — lapis to lapis.

Every instrument of statecraft ran on this chemistry. The oath: gods of both sides co-signing. The dynastic marriage: the bride's gods received honorably in the groom's capital — granting the chapel was not a concession but the ratification itself. The imperial ledger: the Rabshakeh's taunt at Jerusalem's wall — "has any of the gods of the nations delivered his land?" (Isa 36:18–20) — is comparative-deity accounting, and it only works as propaganda if everyone's gods are commensurable entries in one book. Even Naaman, mid-conversion, asks for two mule-loads of Israelite dirt (2 Kgs 5:17) — the territorial instinct so deep that a man who has just confessed "no God in all the earth but in Israel" still assumes worship needs the right soil under it.

IIIThe Noble Gas
Psalm 96:5 · Isaiah 40:18 · Numbers 23:9 · Ezekiel 20:32

Now set Israel's confession into that periodic table — not "our god is strongest," which the system could absorb, but "all the gods of the peoples are worthless idols, but the LORD made the heavens" (Ps 96:5). Every bonding site dissolves at once. No divine witnesses to exchange — who co-signs the oath, when the counterparty's witnesses don't exist? No chapel to grant — what is ordinary ratification in Sidon is high treason in Jerusalem. No exchange rate — "To whom then will you liken God?" (Isa 40:18) is, among other things, a chemistry question, and the required answer is no one: full shell, no valence.

Scripture knows this made Israel structurally strange, and it puts the observation in the mouth of a pagan prophet, looking at Israel from the other pantheons' side of the table: "Behold, a people dwelling alone, and not counting itself among the nations" (Num 23:9). Balaam isn't praising Israel's piety; he's describing its inertness — visible from the outside, like a gas that won't combine.

And inertness is lonely and expensive. That is the exact shape of Israel's besetting sin: almost never atheism, almost always the attempt to grow a valence shell — "Let us be like the nations, like the tribes of the countries, worshiping wood and stone" (Ezek 20:32); "appoint for us a king… like all the nations" (1 Sam 8:5, 20). The prophets already had a name for alliance-hunting, and it wasn't "diplomacy" — Ezekiel 16 and 23 narrate Judah's foreign treaties as adultery, because Israel's whole bonding capacity was spoken for, vertically. The jealousy of God is the exclusivity of the bond.

IVThree Ways to Fake a Valence
1 Kings 11 · 2 Kings 16:7 · Isaiah 28:15

The next three pages are the case files — three instruments, each a different forgery of a bonding site the covenant said Israel did not have. The dynastic marriage: manufacture the sites — Solomon's treaty-wives and their embassy chapels, standard protocol in every capital but his (1 Kgs 11). The vassal oath: accept a bond under another god's witnesses — Ahaz mailing "I am your servant and your son" to Nineveh (2 Kgs 16:7). The secret treaty: swear the oath anyway and take whatever counterparty shows up — which is how Jerusalem's operators ended holding a contract Isaiah could only call "a covenant with death" (28:15): a bond formed to a vacancy.

Against all three, the covenant's own answer was never isolationism — Israel traded, hosted, and welcomed the convert; Ruth's entry shows the membrane was real. The answer was that Israel's oaths already had a Witness, the only one they ever needed, and its security already had a Guarantor: "In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength" (30:15). Quietness was not passivity. It was the only posture consistent with Israel's actual structure — a nation bonded upward with all its strength, and therefore unavailable sideways.

Where the chemistry is headed

Hold the picture through the three case files, because the gospel does not end it — it completes it. The new covenant people are exactly as inert, sideways, as Israel ever was: no god-exchange, no oaths under foreign witnesses, no treaty-marriages. What changes is the bonding model itself — the day is coming when the nations stop bonding to each other god-to-god and every triangle bonds instead to one Center, "in whom all things hold together." Act IV draws that molecule. First, the forgeries.
