The Nations of Isaiah

The hub for the nation studies. Each nation below is a tripod — a family (copper), under a god (lapis), on a land (olive) — and each study opens all three corners, then what happened at the center when the word of the LORD arrived. Use this page to find your bearings; use the studies to go deep.

The compass dials show each nation's direction from Jerusalem — Isaiah's vantage point for the whole book. One quirk: Assyria and Babylon lie east, but their armies marched the caravan route around the desert and always arrived from the north — hence the prophets' danger "out of the north."

A pastoral note. Several of these lands — Syria, Gaza, Lebanon, Sudan, Iran — are live, casualty-heavy situations today, not settled history. The "Today" sections of the studies are descriptive, not comment, and are dated so they can be refreshed. Keep that weight in view on those weeks.

First, the two kingdoms — read this once and half of Isaiah untangles

After Solomon (c. 930 BC) the covenant nation split. "Israel," "Ephraim," "Samaria," and often "Jacob" can mean the northern kingdom — ten tribes, capital Samaria, destroyed by Assyria in 722 BC. "Judah," "Zion," "Jerusalem" mean the southern kingdom — Isaiah's home and audience, spared in 701, taken by Babylon in 586 BC.

The catch: "Israel" and "Jacob" sometimes mean the whole covenant people, north and south. Context decides — but Ephraim always means the north. When 28:1 pronounces woe on "the drunkards of Ephraim," that's Samaria, not Jerusalem.

Code Names & Poetry

Isaiah loves to rename. When a strange name appears and no map will help, check here.

The nameRefers toWhy the name
Ariel 29:1JerusalemLikely "altar hearth" — the city God will make burn like its own altar, then deliver.
Rahab 30:7; 51:9EgyptA mythic sea-monster's name — Egypt as a blustering dragon that "sits still" and helps no one.
Day Star, son of Dawn 14:12the king of BabylonA taunt-song for a fallen tyrant who reached for heaven (Latin: Lucifer).
Dumah 21:11EdomA pun — dumah means "silence." Edom's future in one word.
Wilderness of the Sea 21:1BabylonThe flood-plain empire, destined to become a desert.
Valley of Vision 22:1JerusalemBitter irony — the city of prophets that cannot see what's coming.
Sodom & Gomorrah 1:9–10Judah's leaders & peopleThe most insulting address possible: God's own city talked to as the bywords of wickedness.
The River 7:20; 8:7the Euphrates → AssyriaAssyria as a river in flood, rising over its banks to Judah's neck.
Leviathan 27:1the powers behind the empiresThe chaos-serpent God will slay "in that day" — judgment reaching behind the nations to the dragon itself.
Jeshurun 44:2Israel, God's peopleAn affectionate name, "upright one" — used while forgiving them.
Hephzibah & Beulah 62:4Zion restored"My delight is in her" and "Married" — the renamed bride, no longer Forsaken.
Immanuel 7:14; 8:8the promised child — and Judah as "your land, O Immanuel""God with us": the sign-child whose name holds the whole book's hope.
