Why the two are read together

Revelation quotes and alludes to Isaiah more than to any other book. Not as decoration — its throne room, its Lamb, its Babylon, its winepress, its jewelled city and its new heavens and new earth are all built out of Isaiah's vocabulary. Reading them apart means meeting the same images twice without recognising them.

So Isaiah runs straight through, chapter by chapter, and Revelation is placed against it — not evenly, but wherever the two texts are actually doing the same work. Days marked ★ are the strongest of those pairings.

Two things about the shape

It ends the day before Isaiah begins. October 30 is also the last day of your fall listening plan. You finish reading both books complete the night before the preaching series starts.

Revelation goes quiet for seven days. October 15–21 is Isaiah alone, so that chapter 53 arrives without anything beside it. That silence is the one piece of design in this plan you would notice if it were missing.

Nine movements · daily, weekends included
Sep 1 – Sep 5

The indictment

Isaiah 1–5 · Revelation 1–3

Isaiah opens by putting Judah on trial; Revelation opens by putting seven churches on trial. Read the first week as one long arraignment.

Tue Sep 1
Isaiah 1·Revelation 1
Both books open by telling you who is speaking before they tell you what is wrong. Isaiah summons heaven and earth as witnesses against a people who no longer know their owner; John turns to see the voice and falls down as though dead. Neither book will let you treat its complaint as ordinary.
Wed Sep 2
Isaiah 2·Isaiah alone
The mountain of the LORD's house raised above the hills, and the nations streaming to it. Hold this image — it is the same city that comes down out of heaven on the last day of this plan.
Thu Sep 3
Isaiah 3·Revelation 2
Isaiah strips Jerusalem of her finery ornament by ornament; Christ walks among the lampstands naming what each church has lost. The indictment is specific in both, which is what makes it merciful.
Fri Sep 4
Isaiah 4·Isaiah alone
Four verses of pure promise dropped into the middle of the indictment — the branch of the LORD, the cloud and the fire, a canopy over Zion. Isaiah does this repeatedly: judgment, then a door left open.
Sat Sep 5
Isaiah 5·Revelation 3
The vineyard song, and the six woes that follow it. Read it beside the letter to Laodicea — a vineyard that yields wild grapes and a church that thinks itself rich are the same failure described twice.
Sep 6 – Sep 12

The call, and Immanuel

Isaiah 6–12 · Revelation 4–5

The throne room and the shoot from Jesse. Two of the plan's strongest pairings land in this stretch.

Sun Sep 6
Isaiah 6·Revelation 4
The pairing the whole plan is built around. Isaiah sees the LORD high and lifted up, the seraphim calling holy, holy, holy, the house filling with smoke; John sees a throne, and four living creatures who never cease saying holy, holy, holy. Revelation 4 is not alluding to Isaiah 6 — it is standing in the same room.
Mon Sep 7
Isaiah 7·Isaiah alone
Ahaz will not ask for a sign, so he is given one anyway: a virgin shall conceive. The refusal is the point — unbelief does not stop God from acting, it only stops you from participating.
Tue Sep 8
Isaiah 8·Isaiah alone
The sanctuary and the stone of stumbling are the same stone. Isaiah's children carry the sermon in their names, and the prophet binds up the testimony among his disciples to wait.
Wed Sep 9
Isaiah 9·Isaiah alone
The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light — and then four stanzas ending "for all this his anger has not turned away." Isaiah will not let the promise cancel the judgment; both stand.
Thu Sep 10
Isaiah 10·Isaiah alone
Assyria is the rod of God's anger and is judged for swinging it. This is one of the hardest ideas in the book, and it is the engine underneath everything Revelation says about empires.
Fri Sep 11
Isaiah 11·Revelation 5
A shoot from the stump of Jesse, the root standing as a signal to the peoples. In Revelation the elder says weep no more, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David has conquered — and John turns and sees a Lamb standing as though slain. The title comes from this chapter; the appearance does not.
Sat Sep 12
Isaiah 12·Isaiah alone
Two short songs closing the first movement. Behold, God is my salvation. Worth reading aloud.
Sep 13 – Sep 21

Oracles against the nations

Isaiah 13–23 · Revelation 6–8

Eleven chapters of judgment on named empires, one per day with Moab and the two short oracles doubled up. Revelation's seals and trumpets run underneath.

Sun Sep 13
Isaiah 13·Revelation 6
The oracle against Babylon: the day of the LORD, the stars withholding their light, sun darkened, moon turned to blood. Revelation's sixth seal is assembled almost entirely from this vocabulary. Note also Isaiah 2:19 — men hiding in the rocks from the terror of the LORD — which Revelation 6:15–16 quotes nearly word for word.
Mon Sep 14
Isaiah 14·Isaiah alone
How you are fallen from heaven, Day Star, son of Dawn. Whatever the taunt originally meant about a Babylonian king, Revelation hears it as the fall of something older and reads it into the dragon thrown down.
Tue Sep 15
Isaiah 15–16·Isaiah alone
Moab, in one oracle across two chapters. Note the tone — my heart cries out for Moab. Isaiah does not gloat over the nations he condemns, and neither does the Lamb.
Wed Sep 16
Isaiah 17·Revelation 7
Damascus and Ephraim. Set against the sealed multitude no one could number — Isaiah is watching kingdoms shrink to gleanings while John counts a harvest out of every nation. The same arithmetic run in opposite directions.
Thu Sep 17
Isaiah 18·Isaiah alone
Cush, and gifts brought to Mount Zion from a people tall and smooth. The nations arriving with tribute is a thread you will pick up again in chapter 60.
Fri Sep 18
Isaiah 19·Isaiah alone
The oracle that ends with Egypt and Assyria worshipping alongside Israel — blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands. Isaiah's most startling reversal, and easy to miss.
Sat Sep 19
Isaiah 20–21·Revelation 8
Isaiah walks naked and barefoot three years as a sign, then the watchman reports: fallen, fallen is Babylon. That line is quoted twice in Revelation, at 14:8 and 18:2 — you will meet it again in three weeks.
Sun Sep 20
Isaiah 22·Isaiah alone
The key of the house of David, laid on Eliakim's shoulder — he shall open and none shall shut, he shall shut and none shall open. Revelation 3:7 hands that key to Christ. You read the letter to Philadelphia on September 5; this is where its central image comes from.
Mon Sep 21
Isaiah 23·Isaiah alone
Tyre, the merchant city whose traders were princes. Hold the commercial detail — Revelation 18 mourns the fall of Babylon almost entirely through the grief of merchants who can no longer sell their cargo.
Sep 22 – Sep 25

Isaiah's apocalypse

Isaiah 24–27 · Revelation 9–10

Four chapters that stop addressing nations and start addressing the earth. The nearest thing to Revelation anywhere in the prophets.

Tue Sep 22
Isaiah 24·Revelation 9
The beginning of what gets called Isaiah's apocalypse. The earth is utterly broken because its inhabitants have transgressed the everlasting covenant — the whole earth, not one nation. Revelation's trumpets work at the same scale.
Wed Sep 23
Isaiah 25·Isaiah alone
He will swallow up death forever, and wipe away tears from all faces. Revelation 21:4 is this verse. The last vision of the Bible was written down first here, in the middle of a judgment oracle.
Thu Sep 24
Isaiah 26·Revelation 10
Come, my people, enter your chambers and shut your doors behind you until the fury has passed. A note of shelter inside the storm, which is also what the little scroll and the sealed thunder are doing.
Fri Sep 25
Isaiah 27·Isaiah alone
Leviathan the fleeing serpent, the twisting serpent, punished with a hard sword. Revelation 12:9 names the dragon that ancient serpent — the identification is already being made here.
Sep 26 – Oct 3

The woes, and the day of vengeance

Isaiah 28–35 · Revelation 11–13

Six woes, then the vengeance chapter, then the desert blooming. Isaiah's characteristic sequence, at full length.

Sat Sep 26
Isaiah 28·Revelation 11
Behold, I am laying in Zion a stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone. Also the drunkards of Ephraim and the covenant with death — the chapter moves fast and the tonal shifts are deliberate.
Sun Sep 27
Isaiah 29·Isaiah alone
This people draw near with their mouth and honour me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me. Jesus quotes it at the Pharisees. It is the definitive Isaianic diagnosis of religion that still works.
Mon Sep 28
Isaiah 30·Isaiah alone
In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and trust shall be your strength — and you were unwilling. Then Topheth, prepared of old, its pyre made deep and wide. Isaiah's lake of fire.
Tue Sep 29
Isaiah 31·Revelation 12
Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help and do not look to the Holy One of Israel. Set against the woman fleeing into the wilderness, where she is nourished — the contrast is between refuge sought and refuge given.
Wed Sep 30
Isaiah 32·Isaiah alone
A king will reign in righteousness, and each will be like a hiding place from the wind. Then the effect of righteousness: quietness and trust forever.
Thu Oct 1
Isaiah 33·Isaiah alone
Your eyes will behold the king in his beauty; they will see a land that stretches afar. The question of the chapter — who among us can dwell with the consuming fire — is answered, which is not what you expect.
Fri Oct 2
Isaiah 34·Revelation 13
The skies rolled up like a scroll and all their host falling like leaves from the vine. Revelation 6:14 borrows the image directly. Read alongside the two beasts — Isaiah 34 is the day of vengeance the beasts are counting down to.
Sat Oct 3
Isaiah 35·Isaiah alone
The desert blossoms, the ransomed of the LORD return with singing, everlasting joy upon their heads. Deliberately placed after the vengeance chapter. Isaiah does this on purpose and it always lands.
Oct 4 – Oct 5

Hezekiah

Isaiah 36–39 · Revelation 14

The only sustained narrative in the book, and the hinge the second half turns on. Four chapters across two days.

Sun Oct 4
Isaiah 36–37·Revelation 14
Sennacherib's field commander taunting the walls of Jerusalem in Hebrew so the people can hear, and Hezekiah spreading the letter before the LORD. Beside it, an angel announcing fallen, fallen is Babylon the great — the line the watchman first said in chapter 21. Empires talk exactly like this right up until they don't.
Mon Oct 5
Isaiah 38–39·Isaiah alone
Hezekiah's illness and recovery, and then the Babylonian envoys he could not resist showing the treasury. The hinge of the whole book: everything from chapter 40 on is addressed to the exile this chapter sets in motion.
Oct 6 – Oct 14

Comfort, and the idols

Isaiah 40–48 · Revelation 15–18

Comfort my people, the idol-maker satire, Cyrus by name — and the two Babylon chapters, which land on the two days Revelation is also in Babylon.

Tue Oct 6
Isaiah 40·Isaiah alone
Comfort, comfort my people. The voice crying in the wilderness, the grass withering, the shepherd gathering lambs in his arms, and those who wait on the LORD renewing their strength. Read it slowly; it carries the next twenty-seven chapters.
Wed Oct 7
Isaiah 41·Revelation 15
The court scene — the LORD summoning the nations to argue and the idols failing to say anything about the future. Set beside the song of Moses and of the Lamb, which is the answer to the question Isaiah keeps asking: who else has done this?
Thu Oct 8
Isaiah 42·Isaiah alone
Behold my servant, whom I uphold. A bruised reed he will not break, a faintly burning wick he will not quench. The first Servant Song, and the gentleness of it is the surprise.
Fri Oct 9
Isaiah 43·Revelation 16
When you pass through the waters I will be with you. Also: I am the LORD, and besides me there is no saviour. Against the bowls poured out — the same God, and Isaiah insists you not split him into two.
Sat Oct 10
Isaiah 44·Isaiah alone
The idol-maker satire — he plants a tree, burns half for warmth, roasts meat over it, and worships the rest. Also I am the first and I am the last, which Revelation 1:17 and 22:13 put in Christ's mouth.
Sun Oct 11
Isaiah 45·Isaiah alone
Cyrus named as the LORD's anointed a century and a half early, and I form light and create darkness, I make well-being and create calamity. The hardest sentence in the book. Do not soften it in the margin.
Mon Oct 12
Isaiah 46·Isaiah alone
Bel bows down, Nebo stoops — the idols have to be carried out on beasts, a burden to weary animals. Against which: I have made and I will bear, I will carry and will save. The whole argument in one image.
Tue Oct 13
Isaiah 47·Revelation 17
Babylon as a queen who says in her heart, I am, and there is no one besides me; I shall not sit as a widow or know the loss of children. Revelation 18:7 puts almost that exact sentence in Babylon's mouth again. This is the closest thing in Revelation to a direct citation of Isaiah, and today you read both.
Wed Oct 14
Isaiah 48·Revelation 18
Go out from Babylon, flee from Chaldea — and in Revelation, come out of her, my people, lest you share in her sins. The command is the same command. Isaiah means a city you can walk out of; John means a way of living you cannot.
Oct 15 – Oct 21

The Servant

Isaiah 49–55 · Revelation — none

Seven days of Isaiah alone. Revelation is deliberately silent here so that chapter 53 arrives without company.

Thu Oct 15
Isaiah 49·Isaiah alone
He made my mouth like a sharp sword — the image Revelation gives the risen Christ in chapter 1 and the rider in chapter 19. Also they shall not hunger or thirst, neither scorching wind nor sun shall strike them, which Revelation 7:16 quotes for the sealed multitude.
Fri Oct 16
Isaiah 50·Isaiah alone
I gave my back to those who strike, and my cheeks to those who pull out the beard. The third Servant Song, and the first place the suffering is described rather than implied.
Sat Oct 17
Isaiah 51·Isaiah alone
Look to the rock from which you were hewn. Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the LORD — the prophet calling on God with the same word God uses to call his people.
Sun Oct 18
Isaiah 52·Isaiah alone
How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news. Then depart, depart, go out from there, touch no unclean thing — the second of the two exodus commands Revelation 18:4 gathers up.
Mon Oct 19
Isaiah 53·Isaiah alone
No Revelation today, deliberately. Read it alone. He was led like a lamb to the slaughter, and like a sheep before its shearers is silent — this is where the Lamb of Revelation 5 comes from, the one who was standing as though slain. You read that chapter on September 11; this is its source, and it is worth the six weeks it took to get here.
Tue Oct 20
Isaiah 54·Isaiah alone
Sing, O barren one — and the covenant of peace that shall not be removed, though the mountains depart. Also the foundations of the city laid in antimony, with pinnacles of agate and gates of carbuncles. The jewelled city is already being built.
Wed Oct 21
Isaiah 55·Isaiah alone
Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and he who has no money, come, buy and eat. Revelation's last invitation — let the one who is thirsty come — closes the Bible with this sentence.
Oct 22 – Oct 30

The new creation

Isaiah 56–66 · Revelation 19–22

Both books finish together on October 30, on the new heavens and the new earth.

Thu Oct 22
Isaiah 56–57·Isaiah alone
Foreigners and eunuchs brought to the holy mountain, given a name better than sons and daughters. Then the long indictment of chapter 57, and the promise to the contrite and lowly. The turn from chapter 56's welcome to chapter 57's charge is abrupt on purpose.
Fri Oct 23
Isaiah 58·Revelation 19
Is not this the fast that I choose — to loose the bonds of wickedness, to share your bread with the hungry. Set beside the marriage supper of the Lamb: both chapters are about who gets fed and by whom.
Sat Oct 24
Isaiah 59·Isaiah alone
He put on righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation on his head; he put on garments of vengeance for clothing. Paul takes this armour and gives it to the church; Revelation keeps it on the rider.
Sun Oct 25
Isaiah 60·Isaiah alone
Your gates shall be open continually; day and night they shall not be shut. The sun shall be no more your light, for the LORD will be your everlasting light. Both sentences appear again in Revelation 21 — you will read them there in four days.
Mon Oct 26
Isaiah 61·Revelation 20
The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because he has anointed me — the passage Jesus read in Nazareth and stopped mid-sentence, before the day of vengeance. Beside the great white throne, you can see what he was declining to announce yet.
Tue Oct 27
Isaiah 62·Isaiah alone
You shall be called by a new name that the mouth of the LORD will give. As the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you. Revelation 21:2 has the bride adorned for her husband; this is where she was promised.
Wed Oct 28
Isaiah 63–64·Isaiah alone
Who is this that comes from Edom, in crimsoned garments — I have trodden the winepress alone. Revelation 19:13 and 19:15 take the blood-stained robe and the winepress from these six verses. Then chapter 64: oh that you would rend the heavens and come down.
Thu Oct 29
Isaiah 65·Revelation 21
Behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the former things shall not be remembered. And John: I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away. Also Isaiah 65:19 — no more weeping — which Revelation 21:4 completes with the tears wiped away that Isaiah 25:8 first promised.
Fri Oct 30
Isaiah 66·Revelation 22
The new heavens and the new earth that remain, and all flesh coming to worship before me from new moon to new moon and Sabbath to Sabbath. Revelation closes with the tree of life bearing fruit every month and the leaves for the healing of the nations. Two books that started with an indictment of one city end with every nation walking into another one. Finished.

On the pace

Roughly one Isaiah chapter a day. Six days carry two, always where the chapters are a single unit — Moab (15–16), the two short oracles (20–21), the Sennacherib narrative (36–37), Hezekiah's illness and the envoys (38–39), the welcome and the charge (56–57), and the winepress with the prayer that follows it (63–64). Revelation adds a chapter on 22 of the 60 days.

This is a slower plan than the one it replaces, and deliberately so. Three of these weeks fall inside the Philippines trip, where the evenings are not yours.

Where the pairing is exact

Eight days are marked ★. Isaiah 6 with Revelation 4 (the thrice-holy and the throne); Isaiah 11 with Revelation 5 (the root of Jesse, the Root of David); Isaiah 13 with Revelation 6 (the darkened sun); Isaiah 47 with Revelation 17 and Isaiah 48 with Revelation 18 (Babylon's boast, and the command to leave her); Isaiah 53 alone (the Lamb led to slaughter); Isaiah 65 with Revelation 21 and Isaiah 66 with Revelation 22 (the new heavens and the new earth, named in both).

What this does not do

It makes no claim about how Revelation should be read as prophecy — futurist, preterist, idealist or otherwise. The pairings are literary and verbal: places where John is demonstrably reaching for Isaiah's words. What that means for interpretation is left to you.

Where a cross-reference is noted, it is one you can check in the text. Nothing here asserts a link that isn't actually there.
