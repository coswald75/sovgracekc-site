Reading Revelation

Five skills, and how to practise them

This guide does not ask you to read Revelation once, carefully, and be finished. It asks you to go through parts of it five times, each time with one thing in view. The skills are cumulative: the second is hard to do until you have the first, and the third is not worth much without both. The fourth walks back into the first carrying everything the middle two gave you — this is a spiral, not a ladder. Then the fifth asks what all the reading was for, and answers it.

Scope and sequence

SkillWhat you are learningThe reading
1Understanding the Original AudienceThat this book never stops being addressed to seven named congregations with particular problems.Chs. 2–3, 11, 14, 19–22 (nine chapters)
2Compression, Not CodeTo read a symbol as a compression of Old Testament material rather than a code to be cracked.Chs. 1, 4, 5
3Punches and PromisesTo hear prophecy as pronouncement — condemnation and comfort — rather than prediction.The whole Bible, then the whole book
4Comfort with an AddressThat the comforts and the condemnations were fitted to seven particular congregations.Chs. 2–3 again, then back into the book
5The Diverse ExcellenciesWhy a book about this particular person has to speak in compressed images at all.Edwards, then the Christological passages

Before you begin — get a notebook

One notebook, for all five skills, for however long this takes. Not an app and not loose sheets — something you can carry to church and still open in a year.

Almost everything here produces something. A promise traced to the place the book pays it. A symbol taken apart into the four Old Testament passages inside it. A page where this letter obviously has one of the seven cities in view. None of that is worth much scattered across handouts. Gathered in one place it becomes a record, and reading that record back a year from now is where a good deal of the value is.

Date each sitting. Write down what you found — and also what you went looking for and did not find. A wrong answer you worked out yourself is worth keeping.
