Skill Two

Compression, Not Code

A code is one‑to‑one. You decode it, you get the message, and it is spent. A symbol is a compression — one image carrying a great deal at once. Revelation does not hand us codes.

So this is a lookup study, and it is slow on purpose. For each image below you are given the verse and a short list of Old Testament passages standing behind it. Look them up in whatever Bible you have. Read the whole chapter straight through before you start on its images.

This is for you to learn. Do not worry about getting it wrong. There is no answer key here, and nobody is collecting these. A wrong answer you arrived at by looking something up is worth more than a right one you were handed.
The question, every time
Using what you learned in Skill One about the situation on the ground, and what you have just found in these passages — what do you think this symbol is meant to evoke? What is the compressed meaning?

Chapter 1  ·  Transfer

The vision of Christ

Watch what happens in verse 14. In Daniel 7:9 the one whose hair is “white like wool” is the Ancient of Days — God himself. John hands that description to the son of man standing in front of him. The image is not borrowed here. It is reassigned, and the reassignment is the claim.

Revelation 1:14

Hair white like wool

The hairs of his head were white, like white wool, like snow.
Look up
  • Daniel 7:9
Read Daniel 7:9 and then read Daniel 7:13. Two different figures stand in that vision. Ask yourself which one John has just described — and what he is saying by doing it.

Chapter 4  ·  Assembly

The throne room

Nothing is redefined in this chapter. Three prophets’ throne visions are layered into one room. Isaiah 6 supplies the six wings and the thrice‑holy. Ezekiel 1 supplies the four living creatures, the crystal expanse, the rainbow. Daniel 7 supplies the thrones and the myriads. John is not quoting. He is building.

Revelation 4:10

Casting crowns before the throne

… they cast their crowns before the throne.
Look up
Nothing. That is the finding.
This one has no Old Testament source. Vassal kings laid their crowns before the emperor — it is a Roman image, not a Hebrew one. Not every picture in this book comes from the same bucket, and it matters that you can tell.

Chapter 5  ·  Redefinition

The scroll and the Lamb

John hears one thing and sees another. An elder announces the Lion of the tribe of Judah; John turns to look and finds a slaughtered lamb. The image is announced and then replaced by its opposite — and the replacement tells you what the word “conquered” means everywhere else in this book.
